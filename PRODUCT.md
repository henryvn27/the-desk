# Product

## Register

product

## Users

High school and college students using a Mac or Windows desktop during real schoolwork. A student may be working from a class assignment, source, note, study session, or captured material. Parents may pay for the product, but they do not receive automatic academic surveillance or access.

## Product Purpose

The Desk is the student-owned academic layer that connects the material a student already uses for school. It helps the student understand what matters now, prepare the right context, do the work, preserve evidence, and adapt future study without replacing the LMS or becoming a second source of academic truth.

The core promise is: “The Desk knows what you need to do, prepares everything you need, and helps you get it done.” Success means the student can begin work with the right context already present, understand whether they are actually prepared, and feel that school is under control.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, trustworthy. The product should feel like a capable personal academic workspace: quiet enough for concentration, direct enough for quick decisions, and honest about uncertainty. It should feel native to the desktop and useful before it feels clever.

## Anti-references

Do not look like a generic Electron or React dashboard, an LMS replacement, a calendar clone, a ChatGPT wrapper, a Quizlet or Notion clone, a file manager, or a gamified student app. Avoid giant equal-weight card grids, AI sparkle overload, streak pressure, guilt-oriented notifications, fake certainty, decorative metrics, surveillance cues, and dense database vocabulary where a student-facing decision is clearer.

## Design Principles

- Student-owned: organize around the student's work and decisions while respecting external systems as authoritative where appropriate.
- One canonical truth: project the existing academic graph instead of duplicating classes, tasks, sources, notes, plans, or learning evidence.
- Calm execution: surface the next useful action, reduce friction, and keep secondary detail available without competing with the work.
- Trust through evidence: preserve provenance, confidence, authority, conflicts, and uncertainty in both behavior and presentation.
- Learning over theater: use intelligence to improve understanding and preparation, not to add decorative AI surfaces.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support keyboard navigation throughout standard UI, visible focus, accessible labels and semantics, reduced motion, sufficient contrast, and reasonable text scaling. Keep desktop density useful rather than cramped. Lens overlays must preserve escape/cancel behavior, focus restoration, click-through behavior, and underlying input after dismissal. Core local workflows should remain useful offline, and important inference must never hide uncertainty.
